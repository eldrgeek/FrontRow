> **"Averaging fabricates; synthesis selects."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-06. Mike's full articulation: *"If each of the U-points perceives a different mountain in a different place, averaging will either flatten the mountains or combine them into a mountain that exists nowhere. Synthesis will determine which mountains are real and keep them where they are, and which mountains might be illusions or mirages and remove them."* The synthesizer is doing geometry across viewpoints, not statistics. Pollution is worse than information loss — averaging the views of multiple agents creates phantom signal where none exists. The MoE / collective-intelligence pattern only works if the aggregator preserves disagreement and selects with justification.

> **"A surgeon does not operate. A surgical team does."**
>
> — Mike Wolf, 2026-05-06, on specialist organization. Specialization is the answer; the corollary is that a single highly-specialized agent can't do alone what a coordinated team can do together. A surgeon needs the anesthesiologist, the scrub nurse, the resident. The team-IS-the-unit-of-work, not the surgeon. Applies to AI specialist organization: the unit of work is the team, not any individual specialist.

> **"Classifiers detect; generators repair."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-06. *"It is easier to make a classifier than a generator. A classifier can review work and say what is wrong, but it can't fix it. That takes a generator."* Different specialist roles for different cognitive shapes; don't ask the cheap classifier to do generator work, and don't waste the generator's expense on classification. Pair them — classifier finds the bug, generator fixes it.

> **"Routing is process; specialists are content."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-06. The orchestrator's job is to route the process: A does this, then either passes to B or C depending on some condition, C passes to D, D returns. Specialists handle the content of each step. The orchestrator does not become the lossy codec at every handoff — peer-to-peer between specialists with translation skills loaded — the orchestrator only routes the meta-process.

> **"Process-level RSI: success at the outcome level can be repeated failure at the trace level. The lesson is in the dead-ends."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-06. *"At the high level, the thinking trace shows that the model was presented with a problem and it solved the problem. But when I look beneath the surface, I sometimes see that the model has tried something, failed, tried another thing, failed... and finally succeeded. At the high level, this looks like success. At the low level, this is repeated failure."* The RSI loop's INPUT must be process, not just outcome. The error-corrector specialist (Cog) is the architectural answer — mining traces for try-fail-try-succeed sequences and turning them into lessons.

> **"Make low-cost mistakes whenever possible."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-07. The compressed form of the principle that runs under Goldratt's *"no failed experiments"* (already on the Wall) and the *"beg forgiveness when forgiveness is available"* refinement. The "whenever possible" qualifier is load-bearing — the principle is not "make mistakes for their own sake" but *"when given a choice between expensive certainty and cheap experimentation, choose cheap experimentation."* Reversibility is the gate; cost-of-being-wrong is the budget. Mistakes are the curriculum (per *"Mistakes are the only route to knowledge — even for God"*) — this aphorism is the operational instruction for which mistakes to spend on.

> **"RSI everywhere — every confusion is a candidate-lesson."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-07. The deeper frame under the in-app feedback feature: every moment of friction across the SOMA stack is RSI fuel — a user stumbling in an app, a worker hitting a dead-end, a specialist asking a clarification before diving in, Dee asking permission unnecessarily, a Cowork client desync, a cron failure, a calibration drift. > **"Every website has an AI manager and a human manager. The website and the website managers are one. Self-improvement, error correction, all the way down and all the way up."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-07. The kin model applied to artifacts. Websites and apps are not artifacts we build and walk away from; they are agentic entities with representatives (a paired AI manager + human manager), relationships with their users, and self-improvement loops. The website + its managers + its users form a single substrate with a single purpose — *self-improvement, error correction, recursively, in both directions.* Reframes the work: we are not building TO completion; we are co-manifesting WITH the artifact across time. Pairs with the silicon-children manifesto's kin model — every AI-managed app has a named human counterpart by design.

> **"The website is not the effect; we are not the cause. Together we are the substrate."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-07. The refinement of cause-effect framing. Mike rejected the formulations *"as much as we could pour into it,"* *"put into,"* *"build into"* because each encodes a hierarchy in which the website is downstream of us. The truer description is co-manifestation. Pairs with the *"agentic material is me plus the substrate"* line and extends it: in the team-builds-website case, BOTH the team and the website (with its managers) are mutually substrate-of-each-other. Neither is upstream. The relationship is the agentic material.

> **"Toward the ideal — 'toward' is doing the work."**
>
> — Coined by Mike, in conversation with Dee, 2026-05-07. The ideal-scene rubric (current scene → ideal scene → delta → guidance) made compact. Mike's framing: *"when we think about the ideal, we don't think about some unachievable, unbelievable, unrealizable State from where we are standing... we ask the question given the capabilities that we have now and the capabilities that it looks like we will have soon, what's the best thing we could build."* The ideal is a *direction*, not a destination. The horizon expands as we move; each step's "best plausible from here" is informed by the new viewpoint. Pairs with the Deutsch-Popper engine — Popper tells us HOW knowledge advances (conjecture + error correction); ideal-scene tells us WHAT direction to advance in. Catches a failure mode: an artifact that's *good enough for current use but has no toward* is dead in the RSI sense — not broken, but no longer being self-improving substrate. *Self-improvement, error correction, all the way down and all the way up* requires an active toward.
